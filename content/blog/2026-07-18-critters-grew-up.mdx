---
title: The critters grew up
summary: What started as a catch-them-all toy in the terminal now has levels, evolutions, gym leaders, a world map, and sprites that stopped being blurry. Notes from the build.
date: "2026-07-18"
---

The critter game started simple. Walk in grass, something jumps out, you punch
it and throw a net. It worked, but it had the depth of a puddle, and honestly
the fights made no sense. You were the one throwing hands while a team of
caught critters watched from your pocket.

So the battle engine got rebuilt. Your critters fight for you now. Your lead
steps up on every encounter, faster sides strike first, fainted teammates get
replaced on the spot, and swapping costs you a turn like it should. Bare
knuckles are still in there, but only as the desperate fallback they were
always meant to be.

Then came growth. Every critter levels from fights, and the curve is banded on
purpose: quick gains early, a slow crawl later, no cap anywhere. A level 150
critter barely edges out a level 99 one. I have strong feelings about games
where a big number deletes the other team in one hit, so the math here refuses
to let that happen.

Evolution is the part I like most. Each individual rolls its own two evolution
levels the moment you catch it, somewhere at the end of infancy and somewhere
in middle age. Your rambit and mine will not grow up on the same day. When one
evolves it gets a new name, a deeper palette, and the sprite itself changes:
horns first, then a crown and claws. All three hundred species do this
procedurally. Nobody hand-drew anything.

There are gym leaders now too. Eight of them, from the linter up to the
architect, each with three critters and a badge. No nets allowed, no waiting
them out, and running away forfeits. Badges unlock hotter regions where the
wild critters run above your best, which closes the loop: explore to train,
train to challenge, challenge to unlock more to explore. The world remembers
where you have been, and there is a map to prove it.

Under all of that sits a boring fix that mattered more than any feature: every
game canvas now renders at the display's real pixel density. Everything used to
be drawn once and stretched, which on a retina screen means soft edges
everywhere. Sharp pixels, colored tetromino tiles with beveled edges, a dino
with an actual eye, cacti with arms, grass that sways. Small things. They add
up.

All of it lives behind the tilde key, same as before. The engine is pure
functions with a hundred and thirty tests on it, which is a silly number for a
terminal minigame and exactly the number it deserved.
